# UX and brand case-study patterns

## Job-to-be-Done

Enable an evaluator to understand the problem, constraints, evidence, reasoning,
system, contribution, and impact well enough to trust the practitioner or work.
The case must reveal judgment without disclosing material the presenter has no
right to share.

## Stable structure

1. **Case summary:** challenge, audience, context, outcome, and why the case matters.
2. **Role and constraints:** exact contribution, collaborators, timeline,
   technology, regulation, brand, NDA, and delivery constraints.
3. **Evidence and diagnosis:** research, audit, market or operational evidence
   that changed the problem definition.
4. **Decision logic:** principles, alternatives, trade-offs, and why the chosen
   direction addressed the evidence.
5. **Iteration:** a small number of consequential experiments or revisions with
   what was learned.
6. **System:** the interaction, service, identity, verbal, visual, motion, or
   governance rules that make the solution coherent and scalable.
7. **Applications:** representative touchpoints in context, not a repetitive gallery.
8. **Outcomes and reflection:** measured effects, proxies, attribution limits,
   what remains uncertain, and what the practitioner would change.

## UX mode

Show the problem framing, target users, research method, insights, task or
journey implications, alternatives, prototypes, usability evidence, experience
system, shipped behavior, and user/business outcomes. Report outcomes rather
than activity counts when possible.

## Brand mode

Show the business or cultural challenge, audience, existing perception,
positioning or organizing idea, verbal and visual principles, identity system,
brand architecture where relevant, touchpoint applications, operational
enablement, and effects on clarity, consistency, behavior, or business value.
Do not reduce brand work to logo geometry.

## Evidence and attribution

- Label the presenter’s role and team contributions explicitly.
- Distinguish causation, contribution, correlation, and anecdotal response.
- Connect outcome measures to the original success criteria and period.
- Redact or reconstruct confidential material without implying it is original data.
- Use authentic artifacts with captions explaining what decision they support.

## Primary expert sources and cases

- Design Council, **The Double Diamond** — Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver;
  understand the problem before converging on a solution:
  https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/
- Nielsen Norman Group, **5 Steps to Creating a UX-Design Portfolio** — select
  work for the audience and include context, process, visuals, and results:
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/
- Nielsen Norman Group, **Portfolios for UX Researchers** — summarize findings,
  communicate clearly, and emphasize project impact:
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-researcher-portfolio/
- Nielsen Norman Group, **Stop Reporting UX Activity and Report Business
  Outcomes** — connect UX work to revenue, cost, risk, speed, or retention while
  respecting attribution limits:
  https://www.nngroup.com/articles/reporting-ux-business-outcomes/
- Interbrand, **Brand Valuation Methodology** — financial performance, role of
  brand in choice, and competitive brand strength:
  https://interbrand.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/IB-Brand-Valuation_220608.pdf
- Pentagram, **Pfizer** — a real case connecting purpose, brand architecture,
  visual/verbal systems, governance, regulation, and rollout:
  https://www.pentagram.com/work/pfizer
