# Product roadmap review patterns

## Job-to-be-Done

The deck enables a leadership group to decide what outcome matters, what has
been learned, which opportunity deserves attention, which option should be
funded or stopped, and what can be credibly committed. It is not a decorative
view of a backlog.

## Stable method

1. **Outcome frame** — connect the product outcome to the business outcome and
   state the review horizon.
2. **Progress and learning** — distinguish delivery, adoption, behavior change,
   commercial effect, and unresolved uncertainty.
3. **Opportunity evidence** — synthesize recurring customer needs or pain points
   from research rather than promoting a recent anecdote.
4. **Options and assumptions** — compare multiple approaches and expose the
   riskiest assumptions.
5. **Priority and sequencing** — show criteria, dependencies, capacity, and the
   work displaced by each choice.
6. **Commitment boundary** — use Now for supported commitments, Next for the
   opportunity being explored, and Later for strategic outcomes when the future
   is uncertain.
7. **Decision close** — record approve/change/stop/escalate decisions, owners,
   next evidence, and review date.

## Evidence standard

- Product analytics require metric definition, period, cohort, baseline, and
  known instrumentation limitations.
- Customer evidence requires source method, sample, recency, and whether a
  statement is a direct observation, synthesis, or interpretation.
- Experiments require hypothesis, success threshold, result, and decision.
- Delivery forecasts require capacity and dependency assumptions; discovery
  work should not be shown as a guaranteed dated feature.
- A priority score is decision support, not the decision itself. Show criteria
  and sensitivity when small assumption changes could reorder choices.

## Terminology

- **Business outcome:** a measurable change in business health or strategy.
- **Product outcome:** a customer behavior the product team can influence and
  that plausibly contributes to the business outcome.
- **Opportunity:** a customer need, pain point, or desire—not a feature idea.
- **Solution:** a proposed intervention intended to address an opportunity.
- **Commitment:** work with sufficient decision, capacity, and dependency
  confidence to be treated as promised.
- **Bet:** a bounded allocation made under explicit uncertainty.

## Failure patterns

- Turning roadmap review into release notes.
- Showing RAG status without explaining outcome or decision consequence.
- Converting a single quote into a market-wide opportunity.
- Using a precise timeline to conceal discovery uncertainty.
- Presenting only the selected option and hiding alternatives or trade-offs.
- Calling velocity, story points, or feature count business impact.
