# Corporate training and workshop patterns

## Job-to-be-Done

Enable a defined learner group to perform a workplace behavior and transfer it
to their context. The workshop must begin with the performance need and end with
observable application and evaluation—not with content coverage.

## Stable method

1. **Needs analysis:** identify the performance gap, audience, context, current
   capability, desired behavior, business result, and non-training causes.
2. **Backward design:** define observable learning outcomes and assessment
   criteria before slides or activities.
3. **Adult relevance:** connect content to learners’ work, prior experience,
   autonomy, and immediate problems.
4. **Learning sequence:** explain a small model, demonstrate it, provide guided
   practice, give feedback, then require independent application.
5. **Workshop mechanics:** every activity needs purpose, timebox, instructions,
   group structure, materials, output, debrief, and fallback.
6. **Transfer:** provide job aids, manager or peer support, practice opportunity,
   action commitment, and follow-up.
7. **Evaluation:** plan process and outcome questions early; measure learning
   and delayed application where feasible.

## Evidence standard

- A need is not established by a sponsor request alone; gather performance,
  learner, task, or stakeholder evidence.
- Learning objectives use observable verbs and conditions.
- Practice mirrors the workplace decision or task, with meaningful feedback.
- Attendance and satisfaction are not learning or transfer.
- Use pre/post or observed performance when feasible; use delayed follow-up for
  workplace transfer.
- Report barriers such as missing tools, time, incentives, authority, or manager
  support rather than blaming the learner.

## Facilitation rules

- Protect practice and debrief time.
- Put detailed facilitator prompts, timing, and recovery actions in notes.
- Offer equivalent participation paths for accessibility, language, remote
  access, psychological safety, and group size.
- Use knowledge checks to reinforce learning and gather evidence, not to shame.
- End with a specific workplace application, owner, timing, and support need.

## Primary expert sources

- CDC, **Assess Training Needs: Conducting Needs Analysis** — verify knowledge
  or skill gaps and determine whether training is needed:
  https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/assess-training-needs-conducting-needs-analysis.html
- CDC, **Captivating and Motivating Adult Learners** — use varied engaging
  techniques and adult-learning principles instead of lecture alone:
  https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/develop-training-captivating-and-motivating-adult-learners.html
- CDC, **Quality Training Standards** — needs assessment, learning objectives,
  engaging design, evaluation, assessment, and accessibility:
  https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/qts/index.html
- CDC, **Building an Evaluation Plan** — define purpose, questions, and data
  methods early; distinguish process and outcome evaluation:
  https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/evaluate-training-building-an-evaluation-plan.html
- CDC, **Measuring Effectiveness** — assess learning and learning transfer;
  delayed follow-up is stronger evidence of workplace application:
  https://www.cdc.gov/training-development/php/about/evaluate-training-measuring-effectiveness.html
- Kirkpatrick Partners, **The Kirkpatrick Model** — reaction, learning, behavior,
  and results as distinct evaluation levels:
  https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/
