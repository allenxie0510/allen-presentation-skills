# Learning Agent reference analysis

Status: design-direction study only. The supplied images are references, not
redistributable source assets and not templates to copy.

## What transfers

| Reference | Transferable principle | Observable signal | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attachment 1 | One saturated brand field can create orientation and energy when paired with a quiet neutral field. | Cobalt marks the workshop chapter or active instruction; acid lime marks only a learner action or completion state. | The exact blue/green palette, asterisk shapes, four-card arrangements, or page compositions. |
| Attachment 2 | Training feels credible when real facilitators, participants, and rooms carry the story. | People are shown doing the learning task; photos change scale and crop across identity and practice frames. | The rounded presentation frames, speaker portrait formula, blue glow, or workshop template layouts. |
| Attachment 3 | Large typographic fields and disciplined corporate grids can support authority without decorative complexity. | Short assertions use decisive scale; dense reasoning is held by alignment, numbering, and solid color fields. | The exact gradients, profile layouts, oversized page-number compositions, or branded corporate system. |

## Admissible principles

### Cobalt is orientation, not atmosphere

- Source: Attachments 1–3 and the meeting-room delivery context.
- Intent: make the active learning step visible from a distance.
- Observable signal: cobalt occupies a purposeful field, stage, or instruction;
  it does not wash over every surface.
- Failure case: generic blue-gradient technology slides.
- Override boundary: evidence-heavy or reflection frames may be mostly neutral.

### People perform the learning

- Source: Attachment 2, the workshop scenario, and the adult-learning contract.
- Intent: show employees practicing delegation rather than passively consuming AI
  claims.
- Observable signal: hands, task cards, notebooks, and discussion behavior are
  legible; no staged handshake or presenter hero pose.
- Failure case: decorative office portraits unrelated to the learning task.
- Override boundary: mechanism frames may omit photography to protect reasoning.

### Credibility comes from ordinary variation

- Source: user review of the first synthetic image set and the enterprise employee
  training context.
- Intent: make the workshop feel inhabited by real colleagues rather than styled
  as a monochrome consulting campaign.
- Observable signal: practical office furniture, natural wood, normal signs of
  use, and individually varied low-saturation clothing remain visible across the
  image set.
- Failure case: black table, black or navy clothing on nearly every participant,
  sterile gray room, luxury boardroom, or coordinated wardrobe that looks like a
  uniform.
- Override boundary: a verified client workplace, safety uniform, or explicit
  brand dress code takes precedence over this provisional concept palette.

### Compression creates energy; spacing preserves comprehension

- Source: Attachments 1 and 3, viewed against a meeting-room readability need.
- Intent: create a decisive workshop voice without making Chinese text crowded.
- Observable signal: display titles are short, large, and tightly tracked; body
  text uses open line spacing and strong separation.
- Failure case: every line becomes condensed display type or fills the canvas.
- Override boundary: instructions, accessibility content, and source notes always
  use the readable text style.

### The visual motif is a delegation checkpoint

- Source: the target behavior: goal → context → action → human review.
- Intent: make Agent use feel like accountable work delegation, not AI magic.
- Observable signal: numbered checkpoints, handoff records, and a visible human
  review boundary recur across frames.
- Failure case: glowing orbs, robot metaphors, neural-network lines, or decorative
  grids.
- Override boundary: introductory frames may use only one checkpoint marker.

## Reference-derived anti-patterns

- Four equal cards used only to fill space.
- Blue glow, floating interface panels, neon edges, or synthetic AI imagery.
- Full-deck dependence on one left/right split.
- Decorative lime bursts or arrows without instructional meaning.
- Generic people smiling at laptops with no observable learning behavior.
- Small participant instructions that require the facilitator to read them aloud.
