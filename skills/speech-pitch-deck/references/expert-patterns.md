# Speech and pitch patterns

## Two modes, one communication principle

Both modes require a clear audience shift, a memorable central idea, credible
proof, and a next action. They differ in the decision path.

### Keynote / conference talk

Use one idea worth defending. Open with tension, a concrete scene, or a
surprising observation; establish why the idea matters; develop it through
examples and evidence; address the strongest objection; return to the opening;
and end with a usable implication or action.

The speaker is primary. Slides provide scenography, evidence, demonstrations,
and memory anchors. They should not reproduce the script.

### Investor / business pitch

State what the company or proposal does in plain language. Select the strongest
five to seven facts, then structure around the proposition, problem or insight,
solution, proof, market or context, differentiation, progress, team relevance,
future, and ask. Reorder these elements when a stronger fact should lead.

The goal of a short pitch is usually a follow-up, not a complete decision in the
room. Optimize clarity and remembered proof rather than completeness.

## Evidence and delivery

- Make each material claim traceable. Mark estimates and aspirations.
- Verify scientific, health, financial, and social claims to domain standards.
- Use customer quotes only with permission and relevant context.
- Plan spoken transitions, pauses, demonstrations, and slide changes.
- Rehearse with actual timing and slides; revise content when delivery exceeds
  the time budget instead of speaking faster.
- Preserve a single idea per slide when live comprehension matters.

## Failure patterns

- Starting with history or biography before the proposition.
- Treating emotional anecdote as factual proof.
- Listing everything the speaker knows instead of what the audience must retain.
- Tiny dashboards, complicated infographics, or full paragraphs.
- A closing slogan with no ask, owner, or next step.
- Writing a document deck for a live talk and calling it presentation design.
